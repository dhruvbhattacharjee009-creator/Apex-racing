ENGINEERING NOTEBOOK

Understand the machine

01 / AERODYNAMICS

Downforce is a trade.

More aerodynamic load can increase cornering grip, but the drag penalty affects straight-line speed and energy use.

02 / VEHICLE DYNAMICS

Grip has a budget.

Braking, turning and accelerating all ask the tyre contact patch to generate force. Drivers manage that combined demand.

03 / STRATEGY

Track position has value.

A faster tyre is not automatically a faster race. Traffic, pit loss, degradation and clean air change the equation.

STRATEGY CONCEPTS

What teams balance

UNDERCUT

Track position by stopping first

A driver pits earlier for fresh tyres and attempts to gain time while the rival remains on older rubber. Traffic and tyre warm-up matter.

OVERCUT

Stay out and extend

A driver remains on track while a rival pits, aiming to produce strong laps in clear air or benefit from tyre warm-up characteristics.

SAFETY CAR

A changing pit-loss equation

A neutralised race can reduce the time cost of a pit stop, but timing, tyre availability and restart position complicate the decision.

DRIVER SCIENCE / 06

Driver mechanics & physics

Explore how a driver uses the available tyre grip through a corner.

CORNERING LINE EXPLORER
COMBINED TYRE GRIP — CONCEPT
A tyre cannot deliver unlimited braking, cornering and acceleration force at the same time.

The friction circle (or friction ellipse) is a simplified way to understand combined tyre demand. As lateral force rises, the remaining capacity for longitudinal braking or acceleration generally falls.

BRAKING

Load transfer

Deceleration shifts normal load toward the front axle. Brake balance and suspension response influence how the car uses that load.

TRAIL BRAKING

Blending brake release

The driver progressively releases brake pressure while adding steering, managing front-tyre demand as the car rotates.

CORNER EXIT

Traction management

Throttle is applied as steering demand reduces and rear tyres gain capacity to deliver longitudinal force.
