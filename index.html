06 / Technical knowledge base

Engineering laboratory

Explore the physics and engineering principles that influence a modern racing car.

01 / AERODYNAMICS

Downforce and drag

Wings and underbody airflow create aerodynamic load. More downforce can increase cornering grip, but aerodynamic drag can reduce straight-line speed.

DownforceGrip potential ↑
DragAir resistance ↑
02 / TYRE DYNAMICS

The contact patch

Tyres transmit braking, acceleration and cornering forces to the road. Temperature, pressure, construction, compound and load all affect performance.

GripLimited by conditions
DegradationChanges over a stint
03 / VEHICLE DYNAMICS

Weight transfer

Acceleration, braking and cornering change the distribution of vertical load across the tyres. Suspension geometry and setup influence how the car responds.

BrakingLoad shifts forward
AccelerationLoad shifts rearward
04 / POWER UNIT

Energy management

A modern hybrid power unit combines an internal combustion engine with electrical energy systems. Energy recovery and deployment influence performance.

Energy recoveryHarvesting
Energy deploymentPerformance
05 / BRAKING

Braking performance

Braking performance depends on tyre grip, aerodynamic load, brake systems, temperature and driver input. Brake balance influences front-to-rear braking distribution.

06 / SETUP

Finding the compromise

Ride height, suspension stiffness, camber, toe and aerodynamic configuration interact. A setup that works at one circuit may not be ideal at another.

Fundamental physics

Cornering force

A simplified relationship for lateral acceleration is:

a = v² / r

Where v is speed and r is corner radius. Higher speed or a tighter corner requires greater lateral acceleration.

08 / Transparency

Sources & methodology

APEX aims to make motorsport analysis understandable, reproducible and transparent.

OpenF1

Historical session, lap, stint, pit-stop, race-control, position and car-data endpoints. Historical coverage starts in 2023.

Official documentation ↗

Jolpica-F1

Open-source API for historical race calendars, results, driver standings, qualifying, laps and pit stops.

API documentation ↗

FIA

Regulations, technical documents, event documents and official motorsport information.

FIA website ↗

FastF1

Open-source Python package for accessing and analysing Formula 1 timing data. Useful for future deeper analysis.

FastF1 documentation ↗

Wikipedia

Community-edited reference material for historical background, circuits, drivers and championship context. Important facts should be cross-checked.

Formula One overview ↗

Chart.js

Open-source charting library used to display telemetry and data visualisations.

Chart.js documentation ↗

Data limitations

  • Data coverage varies by season, session and endpoint.
  • OpenF1 historical data is not a substitute for live timing.
  • Missing or unavailable records are not treated as zero.
  • APEX does not claim access to confidential team telemetry or proprietary setup information.
  • Engineering explanations are educational summaries, not official team engineering reports.
  • Strategy calculations use simplified assumptions and should not be interpreted as real team strategy.
